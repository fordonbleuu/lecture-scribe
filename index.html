Frequently asked questions

Short answers about recording, privacy and your notes.

Lecture Scribe uses your browser’s built-in Speech Recognition API to transcribe audio as it happens. The audio is handled by the browser vendor’s service (Chrome uses Google, Edge uses Microsoft) and the text appears live on screen. Nothing is stored on a server.

Everything is saved locally in your browser’s localStorage. No transcripts, notes or audio are uploaded, sent or stored externally. Your lecture notes stay private to this device.

Live transcription needs an internet connection because the browser’s speech service processes the audio. Offline, you can still use Import transcript with .txt, .vtt or .srt files, and any session you already saved is available.

Live transcription works best in Chrome, Edge and Safari. Firefox does not support the Speech Recognition API yet, but importing a transcript works in every modern browser.

Notes are generated locally from the transcript: frequent and meaningful terms become key terms, each part of the lecture gets a summary line, and sentences with definitions or deadlines are lifted into their own sections. Click any transcript line to mark it important and it will be starred in your notes.

Yes. Use Copy notes to put formatted notes on your clipboard, or Download .md, Download .pdf or Download .docx to save your notes as a Markdown, PDF or Word file.
